FMS Movement Screen

Move Well.
Then Move Often.

Quality is a mandatory biomarker that must be verified before progressing to capacity. The period after "well" is intentional.

Principle 01

The Natural Principle

Moving Well

Minimum level of acceptable movement competency — accurate sensory feedback the brain needs to adapt and refine motor control.

Moving Often

Adding volume, load, and frequency — triggers physiological adaptations in strength, stamina, and coordination.

Building capacity on incompetence is unnatural and unsustainable.

Pyramid Profiles

✓ Optimal

Balanced ratio — movement base supports power, power supports skills. The "buffer zone."

⚠ Over-Powered

Power exceeds movement base. Generates force but has restrictions. "Ticking time bomb" for non-contact injuries.

△ Under-Powered

Excellent movement quality but poor power. Needs traditional S&C while maintaining functional base.

◇ Under-Skilled

Conditioned and moves well but lacks technical sport-specific proficiency.

Primitive patterns are the building blocks for functional patterns.

Pattern 01

Deep Squat

The "campfire squat" — not a powerlifting movement. Assesses ability to lower center of mass symmetrically.

Demands

Full ROM: ankles, knees, hips, thoracic extension, scapular stability

Score 3

Torso parallel with tibia. Femur below horizontal. Heels flat.

Score 2

Met only with heels elevated on a 2-inch board.

Pattern 02

Hurdle Step

Simulates locomotive mechanics — double-leg to single-leg transition. Cord set at tibial tuberosity height.

Key Challenge

Control center of mass over a changing base of support — stance leg stability vs. stepping leg mobility.

Inline Lunge

Simulates deceleration, rotation, and lateral movement stresses.

Key Challenge

Symptoms ≠ Root Cause

Low Back Pain

Often caused by restricted hip mobility — lumbar spine forced to move to compensate.

Knee Pain

Often caused by stiff ankles — knee provides motion it wasn't designed for.

Always look above and below the site of pain.

Priority

The Corrective Hierarchy

Focus on the "weak link" with the greatest global impact.

Fix in Order

1
Mobility Patterns First
ASLR + Shoulder Mobility → foundation of motor control
2
Motor Control Second
Rotary Stability + Trunk Stability Push-Up → learn to control new range
3
Functional Patterns Last
Inline Lunge, Hurdle Step, Deep Squat → only after blocks are in place

Why This Order?

Stability depends on quality proprioceptive input. If mobility is limited, sensory information is inaccurate.

Training stability on restricted mobility forces the nervous system to adopt compensatory motor programs that are difficult to unlearn.

Correction

Restoring Mobility

Mobility ≠ flexibility. It's the amount of motion available and the ease of active movement through that range.

Breathing — Entry Point

The most fundamental movement pattern. Primary entry point for neurological change.

Biochemical

CO₂ and pH disturbances

Biomechanical

Restricted ribcage or diaphragm motion

Psychophysiological

Sympathetic nervous system dominance

Tools: Crocodile Breathing · Hook Lying → 360° ribcage expansion

Regional Targets

Thoracic Spine

"Mobile spring" — not a rigid cage. Priority: rotation first, then extension. Rib rolls, Brettzel, quadruped rotation.

Hip (21 muscles)

Multi-planar approach required. Posterior chain: leg lowering progressions. Anterior: half-kneeling hip flexor stretches.

Ankle Dorsiflexion

Prerequisite for every upright pattern. "Ankle perception drives glute behavior." Half-kneeling DF pulses with KB distraction.

Correction

Building Stability

"Instantaneous integrity in the presence of full range of motion."
Most stability occurs at ~20% of maximal voluntary contraction.

Static Motor Control

Transitional postures from the neuro-developmental sequence.

Tall Kneeling

Narrower base → recruits deep core stabilizers

Half Kneeling

Asymmetric base → challenges rotational control

Chops & Lifts: Dynamic upper body + static lower body = proximal stability supports distal mobility.

Dynamic Motor Control

Rolling Patterns

Fundamental milestone — foundation for contralateral movement. 8 quadrants of rolling recalibrate the nervous system "software."

RNT

Reactive Neuromuscular Training — "feed the mistake." Elastic tubing pulls into the compensation, forcing subconscious self-correction.

Programming

The Stoplight System

Never add fitness to dysfunction. Clear guidelines on what to load and what to avoid.

🔴 Red — Stop

Directly challenges a dysfunctional pattern (Score 1 or 0). Loading will cement the dysfunction.

🟡 Yellow — Caution

Doesn't directly challenge the pattern but may have unpredictable effects. Re-screen to verify.

🟢 Green — Go

Safe to load. Underlying pattern is symmetrical and functional (Score 2 or 3).

Example: Failed ASLR

🔴 Avoid

Deadlifts, KB swings, running, jumping

🟡 Caution

Squatting, step-ups, split stance work

🟢 Priority

Upper body training, core work, kneeling chops

Red lights are temporary — "take the car to the mechanic" for a few days.
